# Upstream status data, not more context, unlocks dwell prediction

No public dataset can replace the TPS 2021 event log, and no public dataset can be merged into it to improve per-container three-class dwell prediction in a meaningful way. Everything open is either a port-level or monthly aggregate, which in a single-terminal, six-month window is close to a constant, or data from another country with no join key. The data that would make a large difference already exists, but it is held in closed Indonesian systems. In order of accessibility: **(1)** the dataset authors' unpublished superset of the same extract (June–November 2021 raw capture, unfinished stays, Importer ID, cost and officer fields); **(2)** TPS's own customs-message and billing tables (SPPB receipt time, SP2/delivery request, payment); **(3)** DJBC CEISA declaration data (PIB filing time, lane, SPJM) through KPPBC TMP Tanjung Perak; and **(4)** a 2025+ extract that includes Terminal Booking System appointments. The honest model sits at about **0.41 Macro-F1 at discharge, 0.52 at +24 h and 0.545 at +48 h**. Every model family lands within ±0.01 of these numbers and the learning curve is flat, so the limit is missing information, not data volume or method (local analysis of the TPS 2021 extract). Value-of-information oracles bound what the closed sources could deliver: a known customs-release time reaches 0.71, adding the delivery-job time reaches 0.83, and only adding pickup timing forecast to within ±12 h reaches 0.92. **Pursue sources 1→2→3 for 2021 and treat source 4 as the only route above ~0.85.** Do not spend effort on AIS vessel re-identification, congestion indices, weather, macro aggregates or the Kaggle INCOM file.

## The ceiling is set by information that has not happened yet at discharge

The current extract is not short of rows. It is short of the causes of dwell. Three findings from this session establish that. First, no model family moves the score: LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost, ordinal, survival AFT, sequential-transition and nested stacked stage-time models all fall within ±0.01 of each other. Scaling training data from 25% to 100% moves the +24 h score only from 0.515 to 0.535. Second, the models' own forecasts of the three stage times that decide dwell have median absolute errors of about 29–33 h, with log-scale R² of 0.21 for customs release, 0.02 for delivery-job creation and 0.31 for pickup. Those errors are about half the width of the 65.1 h ON_TIME boundary. Third, context data is already exhausted: IMF PortWatch daily Surabaya activity, the holiday/PPKM calendar and consignment-sibling progress features together added **less than 0.01** (local analysis of the TPS 2021 extract).

An independent analogue confirms the same floor. A Busan terminal study of 394,386 import containers from 2024–2025 reports a CatBoost MAE of **33.25 h at unloading**, nearly identical to our ~30 h stage-forecast error. The MAE falls to **23.71 h once customs-release/delivery-order status is known** and to 1.89 h at the carrier pickup pre-notice ([arXiv 2602.20540](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.20540)). Terminal-only data appears to stop near a 30 h error regardless of port. Customs and pickup status are what break through it.

Indonesian aggregates point to which status matters. Tanjung Priok's INSW stage split was about **2.3 days pre-clearance, 0.47 days customs clearance and 1.45 days post-clearance** ([Bisnis.com](https://ekonomi.bisnis.com/read/20220701/98/1550282/dwelling-time-di-pelabuhan-tanjung-priok-naik-ini-biang-keroknya)). A PKN STAN study of Tanjung Perak's monthly 2020–2022 series found that **pre- and post-clearance significantly drive dwell, while the customs-clearance stage does not** ([PKN STAN](https://jurnal.pknstan.ac.id/index.php/PBC/article/view/2631)). The useful signal is therefore on the importer side: when the declaration is filed, and when the DO, payment and truck happen. Customs processing itself matters less. This matches the local oracle structure, where delivery-job timing adds 0.12 on top of customs release. It also explains why vessel-level data has little value. Only **19.8% of log-dwell variance lies between vessel calls**, and knowing each call's exact class mix in-sample yields only **0.496 Macro-F1**. Vessel call × container-owner prefix explains **34%**, so identity at the consignment or importer level is worth more than identity at the vessel level (local analysis of the TPS 2021 extract).

## A. Replacement datasets: none exists, and the target forbids one

A replacement has to predict dwell at TPS Surabaya, so any foreign dataset can only be a benchmark. The public repository landscape for container-level terminal logs is effectively the single Mendeley dataset already in use ([Mendeley v2](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/yvp2b4rtp3/2); [Data in Brief](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11424786/)). The closest analogue in features is the **Busan terminal data**. It has IN, CR (customs release with DO and DO due date), CP (pickup pre-notice) and OUT states for 394,386 containers. It has no data-availability statement and is accessible only by collaborating with Hyerim Bae's group at Pusan National University ([arXiv 2602.20540](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.20540)). Its value to this project is methodological: it shows customs and DO status cutting error by about 10 h, and it shows how pickup pre-notice can leak information if used as a discharge-time feature.

The other candidates are weaker. The **Veracruz** terminal data (about 2M containers) adds consignee, HS and cargo text but no customs timestamps, and it is processed only on the terminal's infrastructure ([arXiv 2604.06251](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06251)). The 14-port study by **Saini & Lerher** (2.8M entries) has no customs times and is not public ([Journal](https://journals.vilniustech.lt/index.php/BTP/article/view/19205)). **Kourounioti 2016** has an inspection flag and date for 13,733 containers, but secondary sources disagree on its location and it is not public ([Heilig et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.13251)). The **Mombasa** MPM 2025 paper's fields and access could not be verified ([TRID](https://trid.trb.org/View/2709465)). Customs authorities abroad publish only aggregates or trade-value microdata without release times: Peru's import-time microsite reports operator averages ([SUNAT](https://www.sunat.gob.pe/operatividadaduanera/novedades/aduanas/comunicados/2025/enero/co140125.pdf)), Brazil keeps per-DI timestamps internal ([RFB](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/dados-abertos/resultados/estatisticas_comercio_exterior/arquivos-e-imagens/Variabilidadedostemposdedespachoaduaneirodeimportao2.pdf)), and Colombia's declaration files document no channel or release fields ([DIAN](https://www.dian.gov.co/dian/cifras/Paginas/Registro-de-las-Declaraciones-de-Impo-Expo.aspx)). **Verdict: abandon the search for a replacement. A "better replacement" means a better TPS extract, covered in section C.**

## B. Merge sources: per-container Indonesian records matter; open aggregates do not

The open Indonesian sources are all aggregates. The LNSW/INSW dashboard gives monthly per-port dwell, with Tanjung Perak at 3.04 days for 2024, and has no public API or archive ([DDTCNews](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1808265/dwelling-time-tercatat-rata-rata-288-hari-pada-2024)). BPS import bulletins give month × port and HS2 ([BPS](https://www.bps.go.id/id/publication/2025/02/28/51230ea405830664d784ba0b/foreign-trade-statistical-bulletin-imports--december-2024.html)). The KPPBC monthly pre-, customs- and post-clearance series for 2020–2022 reached researchers only as aggregates ([PKN STAN](https://jurnal.pknstan.ac.id/index.php/PBC/article/view/2631)). Over a six-month window these become about six distinct values, which is the same failure mode as PortWatch. They belong in validation (does the reconstructed monthly mean match LNSW?), not in features.

The per-container merges that matter sit in four closed systems. **TPS's own TOS and billing** is the lowest-friction holder. The terminal receives SPPB/BC 2.0 and SPJM messages through the customs host-to-host link. It issues SP2 and handles payment, and DO Online has been mandatory at Tanjung Perak since PM 120/2017 ([PM 120/2017](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/93833/PM_120_TAHUN_2017.pdf)). TPS has released log data for research before ([Telkom University](https://repositori.telkomuniversity.ac.id/pustaka/files/241293/abstraksi/analisis-dwelling-time-petikemas-di-terminal-petikemas-menggunakan-process-mining-studi-kasus-terminal-peti-kemas-surabaya-dalam-bentuk-buku-karya-ilmiah.pdf)). That these tables can be pulled together is an inference and has not been confirmed. **DJBC CEISA** holds PIB filing time, lane and SPJM/SPPB per declaration, with container numbers in the container section. The key question is whether a declaration is still unfiled at 24–48 h, which is the clearest "stuck" signal. Only 5.4% of stays have a red-flag event today, so the lane of most containers is unknown. A 2015 official quote put Perak at 91% green and 9% red lane ([Bisnis.com 2015](https://ekonomi.bisnis.com/read/20150622/98/445959/operator-tanjung-perak-pastikan-dwelling-time-tak-separah-priok)). **Barantin IQFAST** holds quarantine release per certificate and is internal only ([Kontan](https://industri.kontan.co.id/news/digitalisasi-operasional-badan-karantina-kembangkan-sistem-iqfast)). **NLE** holds DO/SP2 transactions, with no public Tanjung Perak data found.

Among the global options, only two carry any per-container signal, and both are small. **BIC owner-code lookup** turns ISO 6346 prefixes into owner and lessor identities through a free fair-use API ([BIC API](https://www.bic-code.org/api/)). It is worth one afternoon. **AIS vessel re-identification** through the Global Fishing Watch port-visits API is free and technically feasible ([GFW Events API](https://globalfishingwatch.org/our-apis/documentation/docs/v3/events.md)). The variance decomposition caps its value below 0.50 even with perfect call-level knowledge. Matching is ambiguous across Tanjung Perak's several terminals, AIS stay times correlate poorly with official data ([UGM](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/penelitian/detail/187209)), and it would undo anonymisation the data owner required. Weather is free and hourly from ERA5 ([Copernicus CDS](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/reanalysis-era5-single-levels)). June–November is mostly dry season, and rain after discharge is future information, so its expected gain is under 0.005.

## C. A better version of the same data is the cheapest large gain

There are two TPS extracts, and neither was published in full. **Extract A (2015)** came directly from the TPS TOS database administrator with board-level sign-off. It has 102,328 cases and 531,524 events, and 16 activities that include customs behandle, quarantine behandle, CFS stripping and Job_DEL_Date ([IJEECS 2019](https://ijeecs.iaescore.com/index.php/IJEECS/article/download/18024/13000)). **Correction to the repository documents:** this is not a nine-month extract of all containers. It is **three gate-out monthly groups (July, August and September 2015)**: 26,920, 40,515 and 34,893 cases, whose earliest events reach back to January, February and May. That is the same gate-out selection design as the 2021 files, with the same right-censoring problem. `data_provenance_and_acquisition.md` §2 conclusion 3 should be amended to match. Extract A is valuable as a second period for replication and for inspection-path priors. Pooled naively into training it would mix a 2015 regime with 2021 COVID conditions and violate temporal validation, so it should not be pooled that way.

**Extract B (2021)** is the source of the published file. The authors collected raw data from **4 June to 9 November 2021** but published only July–September. The source system records Importer ID, which was omitted, and the abstract claims cost and waiting-time perspectives that the 12 published columns do not carry ([PMC11424786](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11424786/)). Prasetyo's ITS dissertation models customs, quarantine and PLP officer performance and process cost, which confirms that the working copy is richer. Its PDF was listed as restricted until 1 October 2026 and still showed no public file on 30 October 2026. It offers a "request a copy" route ([ITS repo 114740](https://repository.its.ac.id/114740/)). The ACISC 2023 paper likewise describes employee working time and service costs in its event log ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/2023/3392012)). A re-extraction of this superset by discharge date, keeping unfinished stays, fixes cohort selection. It adds the importer key that the variance decomposition favours, plus an October confirmatory month. None of this requires new extraction from the terminal.

One fix needs no one's permission. **18.9% of GROSS values are in kilograms (1,000–33,691) and the rest in tonnes (2–40), across 96 of 235 vessel calls** (local analysis of the TPS 2021 extract). Normalising the units is a correctness requirement before any further feature work, even though the expected F1 effect is below 0.01.

## What remains unverified

The attribute lists, periods and data statements of the three IEEE Access papers and the ACISC paper are unverified because their full texts were blocked. So are the ICTS 2019 companion paper's port coverage and the "Effendi 2025, 159,056 cases" paper, which could not be found. That TPS can join SPPB, SP2, payment and DO into one per-container table is an inference. So is the claim that CEISA container sections allow a container-level join. No public LNSW/DJBC research-data procedure was found, and the KPPBC Tanjung Perak 2021 LAKIN was not located. The current INSW dashboard URL could not be reached. GFW licence terms, GFW anchorage separation between TPS and Teluk Lamong, BMKG hourly availability and the JISDOR/BMKG URLs were not confirmed. The Busan, Veracruz and Mombasa data are assumed private because no availability statement was found. All Macro-F1 bounds above come from Gaussian-error oracles. Real status-based forecasters have heavier tails on exactly the stuck containers that define CRITICAL, so the realised gains are more likely to sit at the lower end of each range.

## Conclusion

The problem has changed from "find a better dataset" to "obtain four specific timestamps for the same containers." Open data cannot supply them. The oracle results show that customs release and delivery-job timing together are worth about 0.4 Macro-F1. Indonesian aggregate studies show that most dwell time accrues on the importer side, before filing and after release. The variance decomposition shows that what matters is who the importer is, not which vessel carried the box. Taken together, the actionable unit of acquisition is a per-container status history, and its cheapest holder is the research group that already has it.

The cost asymmetry decides the order of work. One email covering the 2021 superset and the 2015 extract costs almost nothing and gives a valid cohort, an importer key and a second period. The terminal and customs requests carry nearly all of the remaining upside, up to about 0.80. A 0.90 claim can only be tested on 2025+ data with appointments. If steps 1–3 are refused, the project should report the ~0.55 landmark ceiling as a property of the published data, not as a modelling failure.
